Lecture 5. Rigid-Body Kinematics in Three-Dimensional Space

90 minutes. From point coordinates to SO(3), SE(3), twists, screw motion, and spatial 3R kinematics.

Presentation · Python and MuJoCo · Slide schedule.

Main sources: Chapter 4 and Chapter 5 of the original lectures. Additional source: Lynch and Park, Modern Robotics, Chapters 3–5. Use H for configuration and T for a twist. Modern Robotics uses T for configuration; a notation correspondence table appears at the end.

1. Body, frame, and representation

A rigid body keeps distances between material points constant. Position and orientation together form its configuration. A free spatial body has six degrees of freedom: three translational and three rotational. Nine rotation-matrix entries are constrained by orthogonality, not nine independent angles.

A physical point p differs from its coordinate column pⁱ. The same point can be expressed in different frames Ψᵢ. Changing coordinates does not physically move the point.

2. Rotation matrices: interpreting columns

For a vector in frames sharing an origin,

pj=Rijpi,Rij=[(x^i)j(y^i)j(z^i)j].p^j=R_i^j p^i,\qquad R_i^j=\begin{bmatrix}(\hat x_i)^j&(\hat y_i)^j&(\hat z_i)^j\end{bmatrix}.pj=Rij​pi,Rij​=[(x^i​)j​(y^​i​)j​(z^i​)j​].

Columns are source-frame axes Ψᵢ expressed in destination Ψⱼ. Points in frames with different origins additionally require translation.

SO(3)={R∈R3×3:RTR=I, det⁡R=1}.SO(3)=\{R\in\mathbb R^{3\times3}:R^TR=I,\ \det R=1\}.SO(3)={R∈R3×3:RTR=I, detR=1}.

3. Skew matrices and angular velocity

a~=[0−azayaz0−ax−ayax0],a~b=a×b.\widetilde a=\begin{bmatrix}0&-a_z&a_y\\a_z&0&-a_x\\-a_y&a_x&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\widetilde a b=a\times b.a=​0az​−ay​​−az​0ax​​ay​−ax​0​​,ab=a×b.

For smooth R(t), differentiate RRᵀ=I:

R˙RT+RR˙T=0⇒(R˙RT)T=−R˙RT.\dot R R^T+R\dot R^T=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad(\dot RR^T)^T=-\dot RR^T.R˙RT+RR˙T=0⇒(R˙RT)T=−R˙RT.

Thus R˙RT\dot RR^TR˙RT is not a rotation matrix but an element of so(3)\mathfrak{so}(3)so(3), the skew-symmetric matrices. Geometrically it represents angular velocity.

w~ij,j=R˙ijRji,w~ii,j=RjiR˙ij.\widetilde w_i^{j,j}=\dot R_i^jR_j^i,\qquad\widetilde w_i^{i,j}=R_j^i\dot R_i^j.wij,j​=R˙ij​Rji​,wii,j​=Rji​R˙ij​.

Both describe the same relative rotation in different expression frames:

wij,j=Rijwii,j,R˙ij=w~ij,jRij=Rijw~ii,j.w_i^{j,j}=R_i^jw_i^{i,j},\qquad\dot R_i^j=\widetilde w_i^{j,j}R_i^j=R_i^j\widetilde w_i^{i,j}.wij,j​=Rij​wii,j​,R˙ij​=wij,j​Rij​=Rij​wii,j​.

Angular velocity generally differs from the vector of Euler-angle derivatives. A configuration-dependent map relates them and may become singular. We use R and twists without introducing an Euler-angle coordinate convention.

p˙j=R˙ijpi=w~ij,jpj=wij,j×pj.\dot p^j=\dot R_i^jp^i=\widetilde w_i^{j,j}p^j=w_i^{j,j}\times p^j.p˙​j=R˙ij​pi=wij,j​pj=wij,j​×pj.

4. Rotation exponential and Rodrigues' formula

Constant spatial angular velocity gives ODE R˙=w~R\dot R=\widetilde wRR˙=wR with solution

R(t)=exp⁡(w~t)R(0).R(t)=\exp(\widetilde wt)R(0).R(t)=exp(wt)R(0).

Constant body-expressed velocity gives the opposite order: R(t)=R(0)exp⁡(w~bt)R(t)=R(0)\exp(\widetilde w_b t)R(t)=R(0)exp(wb​t). For varying velocity, an exponential of its integral is not generally valid because generators at different times may not commute.

Let w^\hat ww^ be a unit axis and θ an angle. Then

R=exp⁡(w^~θ)=I+sin⁡θ w^~+(1−cos⁡θ)w^~ 2.\boxed{R=\exp(\widetilde{\hat w}\theta)=I+\sin\theta\,\widetilde{\hat w}+(1-\cos\theta)\widetilde{\hat w}^{\,2}.}R=exp(w^θ)=I+sinθw^+(1−cosθ)w^2.​

A unit axis differs from integrated rotation vector ϕ=w^θ\phi=\hat w\thetaϕ=w^θ. For nonunit φ, the same formula is

R=I+sin⁡θθϕ~+1−cos⁡θθ2ϕ~2,θ=∥ϕ∥.R=I+\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta}\widetilde\phi+\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\theta^2}\widetilde\phi^2,\quad\theta=\|\phi\|.R=I+θsinθ​ϕ​+θ21−cosθ​ϕ​2,θ=∥ϕ∥.

As θ→0, coefficients approach 1 and 1/2. The code uses series to avoid division by zero and cancellation. Matrix log gives local exponential coordinates, not a globally unique representation. At θ=π, axis sign is ambiguous; at zero rotation, the axis is undefined. These are not physical orientation singularities.

Numerical experiment

Additive Euler step Rn+1=Rn+hw~RnR_{n+1}=R_n+h\widetilde wR_nRn+1​=Rn​+hwRn​ generally violates RᵀR=I. Structural step Rn+1=exp⁡(hw~)RnR_{n+1}=\exp(h\widetilde w)R_nRn+1​=exp(hw)Rn​ preserves SO(3) to rounding accuracy. It is exact for constant w; with varying w held at the start of each step, it is generally first order. Geometry preservation and accuracy order are different properties.

5. Body configuration: SE(3)

Frame origins may differ:

pj=Rijpi+oij.p^j=R_i^jp^i+o_i^j.pj=Rij​pi+oij​.

Introduce Pi=((pi)T,1)TP^i=((p^i)^T,1)^TPi=((pi)T,1)T:

Pj=HijPi,Hij=[Rijoij01].\boxed{P^j=H_i^jP^i,\qquad H_i^j=\begin{bmatrix}R_i^j&o_i^j\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.}Pj=Hij​Pi,Hij​=[Rij​0​oij​1​].​
SE(3)={[Ro01]:R∈SO(3), o∈R3}.SE(3)=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}R&o\\0&1\end{bmatrix}:R\in SO(3),\ o\in\mathbb R^3\right\}.SE(3)={[R0​o1​]:R∈SO(3), o∈R3}.

A free vector has fourth coordinate 0, so translation does not affect it. The difference of homogeneous points also has final coordinate 0.

Inversion and composition

Hji=(Hij)−1=[(Rij)T−(Rij)Toij01].\boxed{H_j^i=(H_i^j)^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}(R_i^j)^T&-(R_i^j)^To_i^j\\0&1\end{bmatrix}.}Hji​=(Hij​)−1=[(Rij​)T0​−(Rij​)Toij​1​].​

For H, transposition is not inversion, even at R=I when translation is nonzero.

Hik=HjkHij,oik=Rjkoij+ojk.\boxed{H_i^k=H_j^kH_i^j},\qquad o_i^k=R_j^ko_i^j+o_j^k.Hik​=Hjk​Hij​​,oik​=Rjk​oij​+ojk​.

6. Twist as configuration velocity

For H=Hᵢʲ(t), define

T~ij,j=H˙ijHji,T~ii,j=HjiH˙ij.\boxed{\widetilde T_i^{j,j}=\dot H_i^jH_j^i,\qquad\widetilde T_i^{i,j}=H_j^i\dot H_i^j.}Tij,j​=H˙ij​Hji​,Tii,j​=Hji​H˙ij​.​

These belong to algebra se(3)\mathfrak{se}(3)se(3):

T~=[w~v00],T=[wv]∈R6.\widetilde T=\begin{bmatrix}\widetilde w&v\\0&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad T=\begin{bmatrix}w\\v\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^6.T=[w0​v0​],T=[wv​]∈R6.

Unlike H, a twist matrix has a zero final row. Tilde denotes matrix representation: 3×3 for rotation vectors, 4×4 for twists. Code hat/vee converts twists; skew/unskew converts 3-vectors.

Meaning of linear component v

Write H=(R,o). Direct multiplication gives

vij,j=o˙ij−w~ij,joij,vii,j=Rjio˙ij.v_i^{j,j}=\dot o_i^j-\widetilde w_i^{j,j}o_i^j,\qquad v_i^{i,j}=R_j^i\dot o_i^j.vij,j​=o˙ij​−wij,j​oij​,vii,j​=Rji​o˙ij​.

Spatial linear component v usually differs from body-origin velocity ȯ. It is the velocity field evaluated at the expression-frame origin Ψⱼ. For any body point in Ψⱼ,

p˙j=wij,j×pj+vij,j.\boxed{\dot p^j=w_i^{j,j}\times p^j+v_i^{j,j}.}p˙​j=wij,j​×pj+vij,j​.​

Extend this field throughout space: no actual material point need occupy Ψⱼ's origin. A twist therefore describes the whole body, not one selected point.

At the body origin, o˙=w~o+v\dot o=\widetilde wo+vo˙=wo+v. Rotation with w=(0,0,ω) around z through r=(a,0,0) has v=r×w=(0,−aω,0)v=r\times w=(0,-a\omega,0)v=r×w=(0,−aω,0). Points on the axis are stationary, yet v is nonzero because it evaluates the field at another origin.

7. Adjoint: changing the twist expression frame

Keep motion of i relative to j fixed and change only the expression frame from Ψⱼ to Ψₖ:

T~ik,j=HjkT~ij,jHkj.\widetilde T_i^{k,j}=H_j^k\widetilde T_i^{j,j}H_k^j.Tik,j​=Hjk​Tij,j​Hkj​.
Tik,j=Ad⁡HjkTij,j,Ad⁡H=[R0o~RR].\boxed{T_i^{k,j}=\operatorname{Ad}_{H_j^k}T_i^{j,j},\qquad\operatorname{Ad}_{H}=\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\\widetilde oR&R\end{bmatrix}.}Tik,j​=AdHjk​​Tij,j​,AdH​=[RoR​0R​].​

Order (w;v) determines block placement. Libraries using (v;w) have a different matrix. For one physical velocity, Tij,j=Ad⁡HijTii,jT_i^{j,j}=\operatorname{Ad}_{H_i^j}T_i^{i,j}Tij,j​=AdHij​​Tii,j​.

8. Wrenches and power invariance

The original lectures represent spatial force as a row:

Wik=[τikFik],P=WikTik,j.W_i^k=\begin{bmatrix}\tau_i^k&F_i^k\end{bmatrix},\qquad\mathcal P=W_i^kT_i^{k,j}.Wik​=[τik​​Fik​​],P=Wik​Tik,j​.

τ is moment about Ψₖ's origin, not merely motor torque. Angular velocity is in rad/s, linear velocity in m/s, torque in N·m, force in N. Power in W is frame-invariant.

If Tk=Ad⁡HjkTjT^{k}=\operatorname{Ad}_{H_j^k}T^{j}Tk=AdHjk​​Tj, equality WkTk=WjTjW^kT^k=W^jT^jWkTk=WjTj gives

Wik=WijAd⁡Hkj.\boxed{W_i^k=W_i^j\operatorname{Ad}_{H_k^j}.}Wik​=Wij​AdHkj​​.​

For a column wrench this uses an inverse transpose; do not copy that rule blindly to rows. NumPy uses a 1D array, but W @ Ad follows the row convention.

Moving the origin changes moment: a force applied at r creates τ=r×F\tau=r\times Fτ=r×F. Zero moment depends on origin; a pure couple F=0 acquires no force when the origin changes.

9. Screw interpretation

For w≠0, any twist decomposes into rotation about an axis and translation along it:

v=r×w+λw,λ=wTv∥w∥2,r=w×v∥w∥2.v=r\times w+\lambda w,\qquad\lambda=\frac{w^Tv}{\|w\|^2},\qquad r=\frac{w\times v}{\|w\|^2}.v=r×w+λw,λ=∥w∥2wTv​,r=∥w∥2w×v​.

r is the axis point nearest the origin (rᵀw=0); the full axis is r+αw. Pitch λ is m/rad; translation per turn is 2πλ. At w=0, motion is pure translation: do not divide by ∥w∥2\|w\|^2∥w∥2.

Similarly, for a wrench with F≠0:

τ=r×F+λFF,r=F×τ∥F∥2,λF=FTτ∥F∥2.\tau=r\times F+\lambda_FF,\qquad r=\frac{F\times\tau}{\|F\|^2},\quad\lambda_F=\frac{F^T\tau}{\|F\|^2}.τ=r×F+λF​F,r=∥F∥2F×τ​,λF​=∥F∥2FTτ​.

This gives the resultant force axis plus a free moment along it. At F=0 only a pure couple remains. Screw geometry describes instantaneous motion; finite displacement uses its exponential.

12. Product of Exponentials: forward kinematics

H40(q)=exp⁡(A~1q1)exp⁡(A~2q2)exp⁡(A~3q3)H40(0).\boxed{H_4^0(q)=\exp(\widetilde A_1q_1)\exp(\widetilde A_2q_2)\exp(\widetilde A_3q_3)H_4^0(0).}H40​(q)=exp(A1​q1​)exp(A2​q2​)exp(A3​q3​)H40​(0).​

13. Space and body Jacobians

Differentiating PoE gives

T40,0=Js(q)q˙,Js=[A1Ad⁡E1A2Ad⁡E1E2A3],T_4^{0,0}=J_s(q)\dot q,\qquad J_s=\begin{bmatrix}A_1&\operatorname{Ad}_{E_1}A_2&\operatorname{Ad}_{E_1E_2}A_3\end{bmatrix},T40,0​=Js​(q)q˙​,Js​=[A1​​AdE1​​A2​​AdE1​E2​​A3​​],

where Ei=exp⁡(A~iqi)E_i=\exp(\widetilde A_iq_i)Ei​=exp(Ai​qi​). Transform each joint axis using preceding joints only.

T44,0=Jb(q)q˙,Jb=Ad⁡H04Js.T_4^{4,0}=J_b(q)\dot q,\qquad\boxed{J_b=\operatorname{Ad}_{H_0^4}J_s.}T44,0​=Jb​(q)q˙​,Jb​=AdH04​​Js​.​

Both Jacobians are 6×3 and represent the same relative twist in different frames. Three drives cannot independently prescribe arbitrary six-component pose velocity.

Tip-origin velocity Jacobian

The lower three Js rows give v, not ȯ₄⁰. Recover the point-velocity field:

o˙40=Jpq˙,Jp=[−o40~I]Js.\boxed{\dot o_4^0=J_p\dot q,\qquad J_p=\begin{bmatrix}-\widetilde{o_4^0}&I\end{bmatrix}J_s.}o˙40​=Jp​q˙​,Jp​=[−o40​​​I​]Js​.​

At this 3R's home pose, rank(Js)=3 but rank(Jp)=2: two pitch joints produce collinear tip-velocity directions. With nonzero q₃, Jp usually has rank 3. “Singular Jacobian” must specify the task and map.

An analytical Jacobian depends on orientation coordinates, such as Euler angles. Differentiating position gives Jp, not automatically a six-dimensional Euler-angle Jacobian.

Statics through power

For external wrench row W₄⁰ acting on the robot,

P=W40Jsq˙=τextTq˙,τext=JsT(W40)T.\mathcal P=W_4^0J_s\dot q=\tau_{\rm ext}^T\dot q,\qquad\tau_{\rm ext}=J_s^T(W_4^0)^T.P=W40​Js​q˙​=τextT​q˙​,τext​=JsT​(W40​)T.

For W in Ψ₄, use Jb or transform W first. Without other loads, holding actuator torque opposes external generalized torque. Sign depends on the force definition.

14. Examples and verification

  1. Frames and power: examples.py computes Pʲ, twist, and wrench in two frames, checking invariance of P\mathcal PP.
  2. Screw path: rotation about z through (0.25;0;0) m with pitch 0.08 m/rad. Initial point (0.55;0;0) follows a radius-0.30 m helix.
  3. SO(3) structure: additive Euler versus exponential steps at h=0.05 s, checking ‖RᵀR−I‖ and det R.
  4. PoE and Jacobians: compute H₄⁰,Js,Jb,Jp analytically and compare finite differences.
  5. MuJoCo: set q(t),q̇(t), call mj_forward, and compare pose/Jacobians of site tip. This is kinematic playback, not dynamics or control. XML gravity cannot change prescribed q because mj_step is not called.

15. Exercises

  1. Ψ₂ is rotated +90° around z₁. Express physical vector x₁ in Ψ₂. Answer: (0,−1,0).
  2. Why Hᵀ≠H⁻¹ although Rᵀ=R⁻¹? Answer: inverse translation is −Rᵀo in the upper-right block.
  3. Find v for w=(0,0,2) rotating around the line through (0.25;0;0). Answer: (0;−0.5;0) m/s.
  4. Why is a spatial twist's lower part not tip velocity? Recover o˙=w~o+v\dot o=\widetilde wo+vo˙=wo+v.
  5. Derive the row-W rule from power when Tᵏ=Ad_H Tʲ. Answer: Wᵏ=WʲAd_H⁻¹.
  6. Find home Jp columns and explain rank 2. Answer: (−(ℓ₂+ℓ₃),0,0), (0,0,ℓ₂+ℓ₃), (0,0,ℓ₃).
  7. Vary the SO(3) integration step. Why can exponential stepping preserve orthogonality at large steps without guaranteeing accuracy for varying velocity?
  8. Compare PoE and local composition at q=(0.4;0.5;−0.7). Compare Js q̇ with vee(ḢH⁻¹), not just one point's path.
