Lecture 2. Modeling and Simulating a Two-Link 2R Manipulator

Interactive HTML presentation: open slides · controls and interactive experiments.

Lecture 1 derived and numerically solved mass–spring–damper dynamics. We now model a robot with two revolute joints, derive its kinematics and dynamics, and test figure-eight tip tracking in MuJoCo.

Sources are the handwritten “ИМРС2025” material, especially pages 5–10, and the earlier LaTeX notes, Chapter 3, printed pages 10–14. Equations, XML, and Python share one convention. Dynamics includes both center-of-mass translation and link rotation.

1. One system, four different problems

2R means two revolute joints. Our open chain consists of a base, two links, and endpoint tip. It has two degrees of freedom.

Problem Given Find Main question
Forward kinematics Angles qqq and geometry Position ppp and, if needed, orientation Where is the tip?
Inverse kinematics Desired position pdp_dpd​ and geometry Suitable angles qdq_dqd​ How should the joints bend to reach it?
Forward dynamics qqq, q˙\dot qq˙​, torques τ\tauτ, physical parameters Accelerations q¨\ddot qq¨​ How will the robot accelerate under the torques?
Inverse dynamics qqq, q˙\dot qq˙​, required q¨\ddot qq¨​, physical parameters Torques τ\tauτ Which torques produce this acceleration?

Kinematics describes motion geometry without forces. Dynamics relates motion to inertia, gravity, and effort. Inverse kinematics and inverse dynamics solve different problems.

Forward dynamics gives instantaneous acceleration. Motion over time requires initial conditions and integration. Inverse dynamics alone does not correct tracking error; feedback does.

Here Kp=diag⁡(100,60)K_p=\operatorname{diag}(100,60)Kp​=diag(100,60) N·m/rad and Kd=diag⁡(20,12)K_d=\operatorname{diag}(20,12)Kd​=diag(20,12) N·m·s/rad. Compare positions and rates in joint coordinates.

Proportional action creates restoring torque; derivative action suppresses velocity error. For a stationary target without gravity compensation, equilibrium requires

Kp(qd−q)=G(q).K_p(q_d-q)=G(q).Kp​(qd​−q)=G(q).

Thus PD needs nonzero error to generate holding torque. Increasing KpK_pKp​ may reduce error but raises actuator and integration-rate demands.

9.2. PD with gravity compensation

τPD+G=Kpe+Kde˙+G(q).\boxed{\tau_{\mathrm{PD+G}}=K_pe+K_d\dot e+G(q).}τPD+G​=Kp​e+Kd​e˙+G(q).​

Compute compensation at current state qqq. For an exact model and a stationary reachable target, e=0e=0e=0, q˙=0\dot q=0q˙​=0 is an equilibrium. A moving target still requires inertial, velocity-product, and friction torques; zero error is not guaranteed.

This is PD with model-based gravity compensation, not full feedback linearization. The handwritten material discusses the next step, inverse-dynamics control:

v=q¨d+Kda(q˙d−q˙)+Kpa(qd−q),v=\ddot q_d+K_d^{a}(\dot q_d-\dot q)+K_p^{a}(q_d-q),v=q¨​d​+Kda​(q˙​d​−q˙​)+Kpa​(qd​−q),
τ=M(q)v+c(q,q˙)+G(q)+Dq˙.\tau=M(q)v+c(q,\dot q)+G(q)+D\dot q.τ=M(q)v+c(q,q˙​)+G(q)+Dq˙​.

With exact model and no limits, this yields e¨+Kdae˙+Kpae=0\ddot e+K_d^{a}\dot e+K_p^{a}e=0e¨+Kda​e˙+Kpa​e=0. Gains have s⁻¹ and s⁻² units, unlike torque PD. This law is for comparison; the main example uses PD or PD+G.

9.3. Torque limits and control spaces

Each motor command is clipped to [−35,35][-35,35][−35,35] N·m. Saturation changes the applied torque, invalidating direct use of ideal unsaturated conclusions.

The old LaTeX example also uses Cartesian law F=Kx(pd−p)+Dx(p˙d−p˙)F=K_x(p_d-p)+D_x(\dot p_d-\dot p)F=Kx​(pd​−p)+Dx​(p˙​d​−p˙​) with τ=J(q)TF\tau=J(q)^TFτ=J(q)TF. That is another architecture. Here we solve IK, then use joint PD. Never mix Cartesian and joint errors in one law.

In MJCF, the second body is nested inside the first with pos="0.4 0 0", locating the elbow in the first link's frame. Rotating that link moves the elbow too. q2 is automatically relative to its parent.

These fragments belong to different XML sections and are not a complete model. A small positive axial inertia, 0.0001 kg·m², completes the spatial tensor; planar dynamics uses transverse inertia 0.0266667 kg·m². Explicit <inertial> and inertiafromgeom="false" separate physics from visual capsules.
